The endings of the comparative and superlative are `-er` and `-st(e)`:

* **Positive** — `groot` — big
* **Comparative** — `groter` — bigger
* **Superlative** — `grootst` / `grootste` — biggest

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| groot    | groter      | grootst     |
| klein    | kleiner     | kleinst     |
| snel     | sneller     | snelst      |
| mooi     | mooier      | mooiest     |
| goedkoop | goedkoper   | goedkoperst |
| duur     | duurder     | duurst      |

## Examples

> Jan is **groot**.
> Jan is **groter** dan Piet.
> Jan is **het grootst**.

> Deze auto is **snel**.
> Deze auto is **sneller** dan die auto.
> Dit is **de snelste** auto.

> Amsterdam is **groter** dan Eindhoven.
> Zij is **de jongste** leerling van de klas.


The word **`dan`** is normally used after a comparative:

```text
A + comparative + dan + B
```

> Anna is **jonger dan** haar zus.

> Deze computer is **sneller dan** mijn oude computer.

## Spelling changes in the comparative

Adding `-er` can cause normal Dutch spelling changes.

### Short vowels and doubled consonants

Some adjectives double the final consonant:

```text
dik → dikker
nat → natter
groot → groter
```

Examples:

> Deze jas is **dikker** dan die jas.

> Vandaag is het **natter** dan gisteren.

### Adjectives ending in `-f` or `-s`

The spelling of the adjective does not necessarily remain identical to the pronunciation.

| Positive | Comparative |
|----------|-------------|
| lief     | liever      |
| braaf    | braver      |
| boos     | bozer       |

> Ik vind koffie **lekkerder** dan thee.

## `het` + superlative

When the superlative is <span style="color:red">**used independently**</span>, use **`het`**:

> Jan is **het grootst**.

> Deze auto is **het snelst**.

> Wie loopt **het hardst**?

Structure:

```text
subject + zijn + het + superlative
```

Examples:

> Anna is **het kleinst**.

> Deze computer is **het duurst**.

> Van alle studenten werkt Peter **het hardst**.

---
